# Accurate identity-preserving pose transformation

## Decision

Use a two-tier architecture.

1. **Best Quality / simple cloud:** GPT Image 2 or another validated multi-reference editor, with a confirmed identity sheet first and the target pose image second. This is the fastest production path and should replace PoseForge's legacy GPT Image 1 default, but it cannot promise skeleton-exact results.
2. **Exact Pose / advanced:** a pinned ComfyUI workflow with separate identity conditioning and explicit DWPose/OpenPose control, supplemented by DensePose or depth for torso rotation, surface orientation, camera geometry, and occlusion. This is the strongest evidence-backed route to repeatable pose adherence.

Do not make prompt-only editing, generated 3D, or generic face restoration the sole solution. A rigged 3D path is a later complement for hard viewpoints, not the v1 identity source.

## What the evidence supports

- ControlNet explicitly adds spatial conditions and composes multiple controls. DWPose adds whole-body, hand, foot, and face keypoints. DensePose adds pixel-to-body-surface correspondence beyond sparse joints.
- Identity adapters document a fidelity-versus-editability tradeoff. PhotoMaker recommends more real photos for better identity fidelity and supports ControlNet integration. IP-Adapter FaceID Portrait defaults to multiple face images, while acknowledging imperfect consistency.
- Champ's 3D-guided workflow combines depth, normals, masks, semantic maps, and DWPose; this supports 3D as an advanced geometry aid, but not as a token-saving replacement for real identity images.
- MimicMotion supports confidence-aware pose guidance: uncertain or occluded joints should not be enforced blindly.
- Official OpenAI guidance says high input fidelity improves face preservation on legacy models, gives the first image richer texture treatment, recommends a composite when preserving multiple faces, and now recommends GPT Image 2 for identity-sensitive editing. GPT Image 2 is high-fidelity by default and ignores `input_fidelity`.

## PoseForge implementation recommendation

### Identity profile

- Store 3–5 real images per character: front, left/right three-quarter, and at least one profile. Rear views are useful for rear poses.
- AI-generated angle sheets must be marked generated and remain unusable until the user confirms every panel.
- Build a normalized identity sheet for cloud APIs; retain individual full-resolution photos for identity adapters.
- Reject duplicates, blur, filters, severe occlusion, inconsistent age/hair, and faces too small to inspect.

### Pose extraction

- Extract DWPose keypoints with confidence values from the target pose.
- Add DensePose or depth for crossed limbs, seated poses, profile/rear views, and strong foreshortening.
- Validate person count, missing hands/feet, crop, joint confidence, and aspect ratio before spending on generation.
- For groups, explicitly map each character to a target person by left-to-right position and require low-overlap references when possible.

### Cloud settings

- Default model: `gpt-image-2` after a PoseForge regression test; current `gpt-image-1` is legacy according to current OpenAI guidance.
- Draft: medium quality; final: high quality. Match output aspect ratio to the pose reference.
- Input order: confirmed identity sheet/composite first, target pose second, optional pose-control visualization last.
- GPT Image 2: do not send `input_fidelity`; it is already high fidelity and the parameter is disabled.
- Temporary GPT Image 1/1.5 compatibility: use `input_fidelity=high` and high quality for final identity-sensitive edits.
- Generate several draft candidates, automatically score them, and spend high quality only on the best candidate or retry.

### Exact-pose starting grid

These are experiment ranges, not universal truths; run a grid search on PoseForge's own test set.

- DWPose/OpenPose control strength: `0.85–1.0`
- DensePose/depth secondary control: `0.3–0.6`
- Identity adapter strength: `0.65–0.85`
- Steps: `28–40`; CFG: `4.5–7`
- Major pose change denoise: `0.75–0.95`
- Optional face refinement: identity-conditioned inpaint at low denoise (`0.2–0.35`)

Increasing identity strength can reduce editability; increasing pose strength can distort anatomy when extracted joints are wrong. Confidence gates and measured tuning matter more than selecting the maximum value.

## Recommended prompt

```text
TASK: Create one photorealistic identity-preserving pose-transfer image.

REFERENCE ROLES
- Image 1 is the IDENTITY SHEET. Every panel shows the same person. Preserve only identity and appearance from it. Never reproduce the sheet layout or multiple copies of the person.
- Image 2 is the TARGET POSE AND COMPOSITION. Copy the subject's joint positions, body orientation, hand placement, foot placement, camera angle, crop, and framing. Do not copy that person's identity.
- Image 3, when present, is a POSE CONTROL MAP. Use it only for body geometry.

IDENTITY INVARIANTS
Preserve facial geometry, eye spacing and shape, nose, mouth, jawline, cheekbones, skin tone and texture, age, hairline, hairstyle, body proportions, and distinguishing features. Do not beautify, age, slim, enlarge, blend, or replace the person.

POSE REQUIREMENTS
Match the target pose, facing direction, limb angles, overlaps, weight distribution, gaze direction, and person-to-person placement. Keep the anatomy plausible and include the requested number of people exactly once.

SCENE
Use natural photographic lighting and believable detail. Keep clothing from the identity reference unless explicitly requested otherwise. Avoid cinematic grading and beauty-filter smoothing.

OUTPUT
One coherent photograph only; no contact sheet, duplicate person, labels, border, watermark, extra limbs, or missing hands/feet.
```

For multiple people, define `Person A/B/C`, map each identity image to a precise target position, and repeat the rule that identities must not be blended.

## User guidance shown before generation

### Character references

- Use a sharp, unfiltered photo with the complete face and hair visible.
- Add front and three-quarter views; add a real rear/side view for rear-facing poses.
- Keep age, hairstyle, facial hair, and body shape consistent across references.
- Use one person per reference; avoid sunglasses, heavy shadows, hands over the face, and beauty filters.

### Pose reference

- Use exactly the same number of people requested in the output.
- Keep head, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hands, hips, knees, ankles, and feet visible.
- Avoid cropped limbs, motion blur, extreme foreshortening, mirrors, crowds, and heavy body overlap.
- Choose the final camera angle and aspect ratio in the pose image; pose transfer cannot reliably repair incompatible framing afterward.
- For difficult crossed-limb or seated poses, provide a clear high-resolution reference and expect an exact-pose workflow rather than prompt-only editing.

## Quality gates and evaluation

Build a fixed test set spanning front/profile/rear, seated/action/crossed limbs, hands, children/adults, diverse skin tones/body types, couples/families, and all output aspect ratios.

Measure separately:

- Identity: face-embedding similarity plus a 0–5 calibrated human likeness score
- Pose: normalized keypoint similarity (OKS/PCK-style), person count, and high-confidence joint agreement
- Anatomy: hands, feet, limb count, occlusion, and body-proportion defects
- Preservation: clothing, hair, body shape, scene, and prompt-requested invariants
- Product: latency, retry rate, cost, and user acceptance

Do not collapse the scores too early. Following OpenAI's preservation rubric, fail any image with a human score of 2 or below; for PoseForge finals, require both identity and pose to score at least 4. Calibrate embedding and keypoint thresholds on the product's own population rather than adopting one universal number.

## Face restoration and 3D

- Do not automatically run GFPGAN/CodeFormer-style restoration on every output; a cosmetically cleaner face may be a different identity.
- If the face is weak but the pose is good, run identity-conditioned inpainting against the original reference and re-score identity afterward.
- Use 3D only when exact body orientation or occlusion justifies the complexity. Rendered 3D guidance still becomes raster input for cloud image models and does not inherently reduce tokens.

## Commercial-editor lesson and evidence limit

Consumer editors commonly present separate subject/character reference, structure/composition reference, strength, mask, and regenerate controls. Their underlying systems are proprietary, so PoseForge should copy the product pattern—not claim they use a particular adapter internally. The commercial-editor source pass was stopped at the user's request before enough vendor sources were saved; treat this part as a product observation with insufficient evidence, not a verified architecture comparison.

## Adversarial conclusions

- Explicit pose control can faithfully reproduce a bad detector result; confidence-aware rejection is mandatory.
- More identity images can conflict or waste context; quality and complementary angles matter more than count.
- Stronger identity weights reduce pose/style editability, while stronger pose weights can damage face and anatomy.
- Academic showcases and vendor examples are not independent benchmarks. The final engine choice must be made on PoseForge-owned cases.
- InstantID and IP-Adapter FaceID explicitly carry research-only face-model/checkpoint restrictions; PhotoMaker, PuLID, base models, and SMPL assets still need a component-level commercial license audit.
